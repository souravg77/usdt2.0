# 10 Most Profitable Trading Strategies — Nikhil Porwal (2023, Kindle)

Notes on the full text (62 PDF pages; read to the end, including the bibliography). Page numbers are PDF pages counted from form-feeds. They do **not** match the book's printed table of contents (which runs to p. 151): the extracted text is short and every chart image is missing, so the details shown only on charts are lost.
Market: mostly Indian stocks and indices (NIFTY50, NIFTY BANK, Kotak, Lupin), plus US stocks (ABT), US30/US500, FR40, forex (USD/JPY, EUR/USD), gold (XAUUSD) and a few Bitcoin examples (pp. 24, 55). Daily charts for the candlestick and breakout setups, 5m/15m intraday for the opening-range and ADX-EMA setups. The author shows both long and short sides throughout. The bibliography cites ChatGPT, and much of the prose is generic filler.

## Main ideas
- Each "strategy" is a chart pattern plus a confirmation (usually an oscillator in overbought/oversold territory, or a volume spike), a stop at the pattern's far side, and at least a 1:2 reward:risk (p. 10, 12, 22, 28, 33, 42).
- Trade with the higher-timeframe trend: analyse the trend on a bigger timeframe, then execute on a smaller one ("top-down analysis", p. 11, 37).
- Reversal setups are taken only "at the top or bottom of the market", meaning after an extended move and with the oscillator at an extreme. The aim is a small stop for a large move (p. 11, 12).
- Act on closed candles. Entries are taken on the candle close to avoid fake breakouts (p. 10).
- Backtest every setup on history before going live: at least 30 days of data for the opening strategy (p. 34) and at least 1 month for ADX-EMA (p. 42).

## Risk and money management (pp. 7-10)
- Set risk limits per trade and for the whole portfolio using stop orders and position-size limits. Size every position from the stop distance ("calculate position sizing according stoploss", p. 10).
- Minimum reward:risk is 1:2, "so that if your strategy works only 50-60% then still you are in the profitable zone" (p. 12).
- **Daily stop rule: stop trading for the day after two stop-losses are hit** ("Close the terminal if two stop-loss orders are hit", p. 10, repeated p. 12).
- The book never gives a per-trade risk %. It only says "risk only a small percentage" (p. 56).
- Trailing stops (p. 8): the book calls trailing "the golden tool" (p. 24, 28, 33). Start with a stop sized to your risk tolerance and move it up as price advances. The author warns that a stop trailed too tightly gets hit by noise, so use a wider trail.
- Higher timeframes give a better probability of success (p. 10, 12).

## Psychology (p. 7)
- Discipline, patience, emotional control, follow the plan, and learn from mistakes. All of it is generic, with no concrete rules.
- Breakouts are driven by FOMO and greed. Wait for confirmation instead of chasing (p. 21).
- "When the Retailer plans to enter, Smart money plans to exit" (p. 59). Traps exploit late retail entries.

## Strategies and their exact rules

### 3.1 Inside bar (pp. 9-11)
- Inside bar: the bar's high and low both sit inside the previous ("mother") bar's range.
- Entry only on a candle *close* beyond the mother bar (the text says "below" because the example is short; the long side mirrors it).
- Stop beyond the other side of the mother candle.
- Targets are 1x, 1.5x and 2x the mother candle's range (scale out in three parts).
- Rules: R:R at least 1:2, a confirmation such as volume, a trailing stop, prefer higher timeframes, stop after 2 losses.
- Two uses: (a) trend continuation, where a higher-TF uptrend means trading the buy side only on a lower TF (p. 11); (b) reversal, where the inside bar forms at a top or bottom with the oscillator overbought or oversold (p. 11). The oscillator is not named.

### 3.2 Doji reversal (pp. 12-14)
- Doji: open and close very close together, with a tiny or no body. Only plain dojis are used.
- Only dojis at a market bottom or top count, with the oscillator oversold or overbought. The book shows the oscillator only on a chart and gives no name or values ("No changement in the oscillator values", p. 14).
- Long entry when price breaks the doji's high at a bottom. In the example, a gap-up next day confirmed it.
- Stop below the doji's low. Trail it with a moving average (period unspecified).
- Target: a percentage, support/resistance levels, or a trailing stop. No fixed number.
- Mainly daily timeframe.

### 3.3 Engulfing (pp. 15-19)
- Bullish engulfing needs a prior downtrend (judged visually). Day 2 is a white candle whose body fully covers day 1's black body. A doji on day 1 still counts.
- Extra confirmation (p. 15): the day-1 black body is much longer than recent candles; a volume spike on either day; stochastics oversold.
- Entry on the breakout of the engulfing candle's high. Stop below the engulfing candle. Plan at least 1:2 (p. 16). Daily charts.
- Bearish engulfing (short only, skipped for long-only use): uptrend, day 2 opens above day 1's close and closes below day 1's open, day-2 volume above day 1's. Entry when the engulfing low breaks, stop above its high.

### 4.1 Trendline breakout/breakdown (pp. 21-25)
- Draw the line through swing highs (in a downtrend) or swing lows (in an uptrend).
- Valid break needs: a prior trend in the other direction, a volume spike on a signal day, and a signal candle bigger than the previous 2-3 candles.
- Best trendline angle is about 45°. Avoid lines steeper than 60°.
- Do not enter on the line break itself. Wait for price to hold above the previous lower high (for a long), or close below the last swing low (for a short).
- Long stop below the broken level. Targets at earlier swing highs (T1/T2/T3), at least 1:2. Daily timeframe, with a daily Bitcoin example on p. 24.

### 4.2 Darvas box (pp. 26-31)
- Boxes are consolidation ranges, used both in continuation (inside uptrends) and at reversals (bottoms or tops).
- Entry slightly above the box top on a confirmed breakout with rising volume and a strong candle.
- Stop below the breakout candle, or better below the box bottom ("outside the box") to survive volatility and false breaks.
- Target: Fibonacci, earlier support/resistance, a percentage gain, or an R multiple, at least 1:2. Trail the stop.
- "Higher the consolidation, bigger the momentum" (p. 28). Examples: a top box after more than 256 days (short), a bottom box after more than 15 days (long, gap-up breakout). Also shown on 5m NIFTY and on NASDAQ intraday.
- Mistakes to avoid (p. 30): illiquid or trendless stocks; chasing extended trends (wait for pullbacks); rigid rules; ignoring news; emotional trading.

### 4.3 Turbocharged market opening (pp. 31-35)
- Mark the range of the first 15-minute candle of the session and trade the breakout of that range.
- Trade only in the direction of the previous day's trend.
- EMA(14) on the intraday chart: price above it means a bullish day, below it bearish. It is also the trailing stop.
- Targets are 1x, 1.5x and 2x the first 15-minute range. R:R at least 1:2.
- The book also mentions gap analysis and pre-market news, which are not rule-based. One example failed (p. 33): the author shows a loss on a setup that met all criteria.
- Intraday only. Backtest at least 30 days (p. 34).

### 4.4 High-low (52-week) momentum (pp. 35-38)
- Screen for stocks at 52-week highs (using a Chartink screener). On a 52-week high trade only the long side; on a 52-week low only the short side (p. 36).
- Confirm with rising volume or news. Then drop to lower timeframes and time the entry with one of the other setups (inside bar, trendline, opening range, Darvas). The example uses a weekly 52-week high, a daily resistance close, then an intraday opening-range entry.
- No specific stop or target beyond "use stops, targets, trailing".

### 5.1 ADX-EMA (pp. 39-42)
- EMA length 14. DMI with ADX smoothing 14 and DI length 14.
- Long when ADX is between 20 and 50, +DI is above -DI, price is above EMA(14), and the previous day's trend was up.
- Timeframe 5m or 15m for intraday, though the author says it works on all timeframes.
- Exit the long on a candle close below EMA(14). Leave some room between the stop and the EMA so volatility does not trigger it.
- No fixed target. At least 1:2. Square off before the session close.
- Examples show ADX above 30, entry on a pullback near EMA(14), and the stop beyond the day's opening high/low or a swing.

### 5.2 Quasimodo (over-and-under) (pp. 43-49)
- Bullish QM: a downtrend of lower highs and lower lows, then price breaks the last lower high (makes a higher high), then pulls back to form a higher low. Buy at that higher low or QM zone. One passage instead says enter on the break of the higher high (p. 45).
- Stop below the previous lower low or the QM zone. Target the top of the first zig-zag, at least 1:2 or 1:3. Trail.
- Confirm with volume (p. 45).
- Examples: gold 15m, USD/JPY 30m. In forex, analyse on 4h and enter on 30m (p. 48).
- The author says it is slow to play out and did not reach target before an Indian session closed. He says it "works well" in forex or crypto (p. 48).
- He suggests a TradingView indicator for spotting it ("Quasimodo Pattern by Emrekb").
- Bearish QM is short only.

### 5.3 Price-action traps (pp. 49-59)
- Bull trap (short only): a break of the day high or resistance that fails, then a red candle on a volume spike. Skipped for long-only use.
- **Bear trap (long):** price breaks below significant support, then quickly closes back above it. Confirmation: low volume on the breakdown, then a volume spike on the reclaim, a strong bullish candle, or positive divergence.
- Buy above the recent swing high or above the high of the bullish reversal candle. Stop below the recent swing low or the low of the breakdown candle.
- Targets: previous resistance, the day high, the previous day's high, or Fibonacci extensions. Take partial profits and trail.
- The examples are intraday (NIFTY, Bank NIFTY, EUR/USD, HDFC Bank).

## What the author says does not work or to avoid
- Entering on the first touch of a breakout or breakdown instead of a candle close or a hold beyond the swing (p. 10, 22).
- Trading inside bars in a range-bound market (less significant, p. 9). Not every doji is a reversal (p. 12).
- Trading against the higher-timeframe or previous-day trend (p. 22, 33, 42).
- Trendlines steeper than 60° (p. 22).
- Chasing extended Darvas moves, and trading illiquid names (p. 30).
- Quasimodo on short-session markets, because it is too slow (p. 48).
- Continuing to trade after 2 losing trades in a day (p. 10).

## Claims to treat with caution
- The title's "most profitable" and the intro's "proven to be effective" (p. 6) come with no statistics, win rates or backtest results anywhere in the book.
- "Maximum profit against small stop losses" for reversal inside bars (p. 11): based on cherry-picked chart examples.
- "Nifty falls ... more than 14%" and "FR40EUR falls more than 16%" after bearish engulfing (p. 19): single anecdotes.
- "All the strategies perfectly worked in this stock" (p. 37): a single example.
- "When you backtest this strategy ... you will get thousands of traps" (p. 54): no test data is given.
- Quasimodo "works well" in forex/crypto (p. 48): the author gives no evidence.
- The 1:2 R:R with "50-60%" win rate is arithmetic, not measured performance.
- Much of the text appears AI-generated (the bibliography cites ChatGPT), and the specifics behind several rules (oscillator settings, exact entry points) appear only in images that are missing from the extracted text.
