# Day Trading Guide For Beginners (Warrior Trading / Ross Cameron and team)

Market: US equities, mainly small-cap "penny"/momentum stocks intraday (1m/5m charts), with some large-cap and
daily-chart examples (NVDA, SPY, ADP, AAPL, AMGN, EW) and a passing gold/forex example. Not crypto.
Page citations use the book's printed page numbers (the number that heads each extracted page; they match the
table of contents). 68 PDF pages; printed pages run 4-67. Most of the book is broker/tool marketing; the trading rules
sit in Ch. 4 (p. 24-25), Ch. 5 (p. 33-36), Ch. 6 (p. 36-48) and Ch. 7.2 (p. 55-57).

## Main ideas
- Day trading means opening and closing a position in the same session. Traders want volatility and treat the job
  as managing risk, with entry, stop and exit decided before the trade (p. 5).
- Before trading real money you need three things: knowledge, a tested strategy that has made money, and proof of
  profit in a simulator. "If you can't make money in a trading simulator" you probably can't live (p. 6-8).
- Master one setup first. Running 5 strategies at once hides which ones make money and which lose (p. 7).
- Daily routine: written plan reviewed each morning, a morning watchlist, trade the plan, review trades at day end.
  Go live slowly and do not trade max size at first (p. 8).
- Momentum is "what day trading is all about". The edge is finding the handful of stocks moving 20-30% that day (p. 34).
- Trade with the trend; fighting it is costly (p. 24). Confirm across time frames (p. 40-45).
- Disclaimer (p. 3) cites studies: fewer than 3% of day traders are predictably profitable (Taiwan) and 35-50% of
  accounts were profitable in older US samples. The authors say at least 50% of aspiring day traders lose.

## Charts and basic concepts (p. 21-28)
- Popular intraday time frames are 1, 5 and 15 minutes. Swing traders use 30m, 60m, daily and weekly (p. 21).
- The example chart shows a 9-period exponential moving average (p. 22).
- Candles: the body is open to close, wicks are the high and low, green means close > open. A long body shows strong
  pressure and a small body light pressure. The book names hammers and shooting stars but gives no rules for them (p. 23, 26-27).
- A 5-minute regular session has 78 candles (p. 28).
- **Trend rule (p. 24):** in an uptrend, buy pullbacks to support or a moving average. Stay in until you get
  *consecutive closes below* a support level or moving average.
- **Support/resistance (p. 25):** buy at support; a break below support means you are wrong, so exit. This gives a
  defined stop and a good R/R.
- **Breakout (p. 25):** a defined level forms and price breaks through it. Look for (a) high relative volume versus
  earlier periods and (b) price holding above the level. The ideal entry is a break, a retest of the level and a hold.
- Buy-stop entries just above resistance catch shorts covering (p. 20).

## Order types (p. 18-20)
- Limit orders are the default. Use market orders only in emergencies, such as bailing out fast, because of slippage
  in thin books (p. 19).
- A stop-market order turns into a market order when hit. Sell-stops protect longs (p. 20).

## Strategies (Ch. 5)
### Penny / small-cap momentum (p. 33-34)
- Universe: about $5 stocks listed on NYSE/NASDAQ.
- Filters: (1) news catalyst gap-up (FDA, earnings), but avoid buyout targets; (2) float < 100M shares, < 50M better;
  (3) high relative volume, active premarket.
- Entries: a breakout over premarket highs at the open, or a bull flag.
- **Bull flag (p. 34):** a run up, then a *light-volume* pullback, usually to the 10 or 20 MA on the 1 or 5 min chart,
  then entry when volume picks back up. The risk point is clearly defined (the pullback low).
- **Flat-top breakout over premarket highs (p. 34):** shorts' buy-stops sit above the highs and push price higher once it breaks.
### Momentum candidate criteria (p. 34)
1. Float < 100M shares. 2. Strong daily chart: above the moving averages, no resistance nearby. 3. Relative volume
   at least 2x the average for that time of day. 4. A fundamental catalyst (PR, earnings, FDA, activist). Without one it
   is a "technical breakout".
- Entry: strong moves right at the open, or a micro-pullback forming a bull flag. The breakout candle pushes through
  the level on high volume. The best window is 9:30-10:30 AM ET (p. 12).
### Reversal (p. 35-36)
- Criteria: (1) new intraday high or low; (2) at least 5-10 consecutive 5-min candles of the same color; (3) ideally
  RSI < 10 or > 90. The idea is a stretched "rubber band".
- Buy the first candle that starts to reverse. Stop at the low (or high) or 20 cents beyond it. Use trailing stops.
- Short example: break below the low of a bearish spinning top, stop above the high, trailing stop (p. 36).
- The book warns that reversals are hard to time, fight the trend and suit more experienced traders.

## Position sizing and stops (Ch. 6.1, p. 36-40)
- Percent-of-portfolio risk is the most common method. 1% of $25k = $250. Larger accounts risk a smaller %.
  Alternative: a fixed dollar amount per trade (p. 36-37).
- Always have a defined exit and size the trade from it: shares = dollar risk / stop distance (p. 37).
- **ATR stop (p. 37-38):** for years the standard was 2x ATR. Raschke says noise has grown, so the book uses
  **2.5x ATR**. Worked example: NVDA ATR $5.95, stop $14.87 wide, 1% of $100k = $1,000 risk, 62 shares.
- **Standard-deviation stop (p. 38-39):** Bollinger Bands 2 SD around a 20 SMA. For a multi-month SPY long, use the
  lower band of a 2 SD band around the **50 SMA** as the stop. Example: $14.23 stop, 70 shares.
- **Vital-level stop (p. 39-40):** a stop at support gives asymmetric R/R. Warning: many "levels" are just random
  prices that traded a few times. ADP example: $7.90 stop, 126 shares.
- **Percent-loss stop (p. 40):** exit if the stock drops X% below entry. Mostly used by swing traders.

## Multiple time frames (Ch. 6.2, p. 40-45)
- Confirm a lower-TF setup against higher TFs. Do not short a 15m downtrend when the weekly is a strong uptrend (EW
  example), and do not fade 5m breakouts while the hourly and daily make higher highs and lows (AAPL).
- Pair similar TFs, for example 5m with 1h/2h (not monthly), or 1m with 30m. Ross uses daily + 15m panes (p. 57).
- Levels on daily/weekly charts matter far more than 5-min levels (the AMGN $177 level was irrelevant on the daily) (p. 43-44).
- Most traders win about half their trades, so average winners must be larger than average losers (p. 41).
- Pareto: about 80% of returns come from 20% of trades, so let winners run (p. 42).

## Risk management math (Ch. 6.3, p. 46-48)
- Set the stop and take-profit before entry. Example: 9 trades/day with 5 wins x $100 and 4 losses x $100 = +$100.
  With 4 wins x $200 and 5 losses x $150 = +$50 (p. 46).
- Know the instrument's average daily range. Backtest (even manually), then test in real time (p. 47).
- Stops can be % based, $ based or below a chart point. Use trend lines or MAs for S/R (p. 47).
- Example: buy when RSI moves into oversold and take profit at the 50-period MA, with the stop on a trend line (p. 48).
- Bollinger Bands can define take-profit and stop levels intraday (p. 48).

## Shorting, premarket, short squeezes (p. 49-61)
- Shorting needs margin and a borrow (locate/HTB). This does not apply to our long-only spot bot.
- Premarket (4:00-9:30 ET) is illiquid, accepts limit orders only, has wide spreads and extreme volatility. Beginners
  should avoid it. Premarket trend and range can reverse after the open (p. 52-54).
- Short squeeze: heavily shorted stock + catalyst leads to forced covering and a parabolic move. SIR = shares short /
  average daily volume (p. 58-61). Shorts should always use hard stops.

## Watchlist / trend-pullback plan (Ch. 7.2, p. 55-57)
- Pick 2-3 high-conviction setups and write each one down.
- **Trend pullback plan (p. 55):** criteria are higher highs and higher lows with strong momentum and volume. Do
  not enter on a dramatic pullback (it may be a reversal). Green flag: a shallow pullback into a consolidation on
  light volume. Stop at a multiple of the TF's ATR or the recent swing low.
- Example universe filters: market cap < $2B, float < 50M, price > $1, average volume > 100K/day (p. 56).
- Broad screen: price > 20-day SMA, price 0-5% below the 50-day high, down on the day (p. 57).
- Narrow screen: price > 20-day SMA, closed above the 5-day SMA for 7+ days in a row but closed below it today (p. 57).
- More specific screens give fewer but more accurate hits. You have to test which works (p. 57).

## Psychology (Ch. 7.4, p. 62-64)
- FOMO leads to chasing and poor entries. Fix: rules plus a penalty for breaking them, such as no more trading that day.
- Fear: trade within your means and fix the acceptable loss before entry. Too much size causes fear, so increase
  size gradually (not 500 shares to 5,000 after one good month).
- Greed: set profit targets in advance and take profit when they hit. Do not squeeze every penny out of a move.
- Hope clouds judgment. Be "like a robot": systematic rules for finding trades, executing them and cooling down afterwards.
- Ross stopped trading for the day whenever he broke a rule.

## What the book says does not work / avoid
- Fighting the trend, and fading moves inside a higher-TF uptrend (p. 24, 45).
- Market orders in thin stocks (slippage). Trading premarket as a beginner.
- Stocks being bought out, because they pin to the deal price (p. 33).
- Chasing after a missed move (FOMO). Learning 100 strategies a little.
- Dramatic pullbacks in a trend-pullback setup, which may be reversals (p. 55).
- Treating every repeated price as support (p. 39).

## Claims to treat with caution
- Ross turned $500 into over $1 million in two years (p. 9). The book itself says this is atypical.
- "Almost every single day there is a stock that will move 20-30% or even more! This is a fact." (p. 34)
- "Proven" or "profitable" strategies (p. 7, 12): no statistics, win rates or backtests are given for any setup.
- Both the reversal short and the bull-flag long "would have been successful" on a chart example (p. 35). This is hindsight.
- Breakouts over premarket highs work because short stops trigger (p. 34). This mechanism is asserted, not measured.
- Disclaimer studies (p. 3) are cited correctly, but they argue *against* the typical student succeeding.
