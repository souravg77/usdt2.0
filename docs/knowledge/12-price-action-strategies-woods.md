# How to Trade with Price Action (Strategies Edition) — Galen Woods, 2014

Source id: 9074f83b. Read in full, pp. 2–62. A compilation of 10 short articles from the
TradingSetupsReview website. **Market:** mostly US index futures (YM, ES, CL) on intraday charts
(3-, 4- and 5-minute). Stock, ETF and forex examples use daily, weekly or monthly charts. Nothing
in the book is about crypto. All charts are from NinjaTrader. The extracted text has many words
run together without spaces (a PDF artifact). The chart images are missing, so only the captions
are left.

## Main ideas
- Price action setups with very few indicators: a single SMA or EMA (20/21), plus volume in the last chapter (p. 9).
- **Trade with the trend and against the trapped traders.** Most of the setups are continuation entries. They work when counter-trend traders get trapped and have to cover (pp. 32, 42, 55).
- **Avoid congestion.** In almost every chapter, the losing example is a setup that formed inside a sideways range. Inside bars, NR7 bars, dojis and engulfing bars all lose reliability there (pp. 14, 29, 51, 55).
- Entries are usually a **stop order one tick beyond the signal bar**. This needs the market to confirm the move before you are in.
- "Strategies alone are useless … They trade the market" (p. 61). Context counts for more than the rules.
- Trend definitions: higher highs and higher lows (market structure), or the slope of the 20 EMA (p. 30). "When it is doubtful whether the market is trending, it is not trending" (p. 30).

## Time-frame
- The author uses his own "Price Action Time-frame Index" (PATI) to find the minimum tradeable time-frame (MTT). The book never explains how. For YM futures it came out at 4 minutes (pp. 10–11). A higher time-frame gives fewer setups.
- 5-minute is called the usual day-trading choice, and it is Al Brooks's "sweet spot" (p. 41).

## The setups (long side; each one is mirrored for shorts)
1. **Inside bar after a 21 SMA cross** (pp. 11–15). A bar clears completely above the 21-period SMA, coming from below. Wait for the *first* bullish inside bar, then buy stop one tick above it. Aim for at least the last extreme of the trend. Other target options are S/R from swing pivots and price thrusts, or the prior session's high or low. The winning example made 2:1. Look for *new* trends and avoid congestion: sideways bars with shadows on both ends.
2. **Four or more consecutive down days, swing low held** (pp. 17–20). Four or more down days in a row (close below open). If the last swing low was not breached, buy stop one tick above the next bullish bar. Market structure filters out the counter-trend cases. Related patterns from his course are "Deceleration" (the thrust weakens, which is good) and "Anti-climax" (an exhaustive thrust). The book says studies that trade consecutive days blindly are not conclusive. Note that the short rule on p. 17 has a typo ("buy a tick above"); the example sells below a bearish bar.
3. **Enhanced three-bar reversal** (after Alton Hill, pp. 21–25). Bar 1 closes down. Bar 2's low is below the lows of bars 1 and 3. Bar 3 *closes above the highs* of both bar 1 and bar 2. Buy at bar 3's close. The stop usually goes at the far end of the signal bar. That bar is often large, so cut size or tighten the stop, or skip the trade. Be careful when bar 2 is an outside bar.
4. **NR7 with an EMA trend** (pp. 26–29). NR7 is a bar with a smaller range than each of the 6 bars before it. The last 7 bars must be completely above the 20 EMA. Buy on a break of the NR7 high. In the example the stop was at the NR7's opposite extreme. Re-entry after a whipsaw is allowed if the trend still holds. Skip NR7 bars at the trend extreme (wait for a real pullback). Be wary of several NR7 bars close together, which signals congestion. The setup traces back to Toby Crabel's NR7 and NR4/ID work.
5. **Trend Bar Failure** (pp. 30–38). Trend filter: the 20 EMA is sloping up. A *trend bar* has a body larger than 50% of its range. The setup is a bear trend bar whose low is then broken without a second bear trend bar forming. Put a buy stop above the high of that breaking bar and cancel it if it does not fill within 1 bar. The book says "the best trades happen quickly" (p. 34). Examples run from 5-minute ES to a monthly SPY chart. Related forms are the inside-bar failure and the "pipe" pattern.
6. **Two-legged pullback to the 20 EMA (M2B, from Al Brooks)** (pp. 39–42). Needs a strong uptrend. Wait for a two-legged pullback to the 20 EMA, then buy one tick above the bar that tested the EMA. Leg counting: a bar with a higher high starts a new up-leg, and a bar with a lower low starts a new down-leg. The winning case showed clear rejection at the EMA. The losing case's trend had bear trend bars inside it. Very strong trends often pull back in a single leg, so waiting for two legs means missing some trades.
7. **High-close doji at a pivot support** (after John Person, pp. 43–47). Floor pivots: PP=(H+L+C)/3, S1=2PP−H, S2=PP−(H−L), R1=2PP−L, R2=PP+(H−L), all from the prior session. A high-close doji is a doji followed by a bar that closes above the doji's high. Rule: a doji at a support level, then buy on the high-close doji's close. S1 is the natural first target after an S2 entry. Better setups have multiple dojis at the level, no strong bearish bars, and a failed low-close doji before them. "Most break-outs fail." Pivots are no better than any other S/R.
8. **Bullish engulfing with market structure** (pp. 48–51). Needs a higher swing high and a higher swing low. Buy on a bullish engulfing (the second body engulfs the first). The pattern's high must stay below the previous swing high, which keeps room for reward. Do *not* wait for a confirmation bar, because that hurts R:R. Avoid sideways markets.
9. **Hikkake** (Dan Chesler, Active Trader 2004; pp. 52–55). An inside bar, then a bar with a lower high and a lower low, which is the false breakdown. Place a buy order at the inside bar's high, good for the next 3 bars, and cancel it after that. It works best when many breakout traders are trapped. The setup often fails when an outside bar triggers it.
10. **Low-volume pullback** (pp. 56–60). Price is above the 20 SMA. It breaks above the last swing high on higher volume, then falls back on lower volume. Buy one tick above the next bullish bar. "Lower" volume means lower than the previous bar; the author says absolute volume levels are useless. Check that no larger resistance overhead caps the move (the losing Walmart example). This filter can be added to other pullback setups (Holy Grail, two-legged pullback, 9/30).

## Risk and money management
- The book gives **no position-size or %-risk figures**. Stops are structural: the opposite end of the signal or setup bar.
- If the signal bar is large, reduce size, tighten the stop, or skip the trade (p. 24).
- Targets: at least the last trend extreme, which gave 2:1 in the example (p. 13). Other targets are S/R and the prior session's high or low. S1 is the target after an S2 pivot entry. "10 Ways to Take Profit" is cited but not included in the book.
- Pending orders expire: after 1 bar for Trend Bar Failure, after 3 bars for Hikkake.

## Trade management
- Mostly entry-focused. Exits are only touched on: a conservative trader might exit during sideways congestion after entry (p. 36). Re-entering after a stop-out is valid if the trend context holds (NR7, p. 28).
- Trades can take a long time to play out. The weekly ETF short took 15 weeks (p. 58).

## Psychology and concepts
- Gambler's fallacy: traders expect a streak to reverse (p. 16).
- Trapped traders: "You always profit at other traders' loss" (p. 55).
- Do not memorize setups without the idea behind them (p. 30). Do not follow NR7 rules mechanically (p. 29).
- When in doubt, wait for more price action (p. 47).

## What the author says doesn't work
- Candlesticks used on their own (p. 43). Engulfing patterns as major reversal signals are "low probability" (p. 50).
- Trading consecutive up or down days without a filter (p. 20). Trading pivot reversals without discrimination (p. 47).
- Inside bars and NR7s inside congestion, and NR7 used for reversals without confirmation (pp. 14, 27, 29).
- Waiting for confirmation after an engulfing bar hurts R:R (p. 51).
- Outside bars bring unpredictable action (pp. 24, 55).

## Claims to treat with caution
- "High positive expectancy" for the inside-bar setup is claimed from a single 2:1 example (p. 13).
- The enhanced three-bar reversal is called a "powerful setup" with no statistics (p. 24).
- Two-legged pullbacks are called "high probability" and among "the most reliable" setups, appealing to Al Brooks (pp. 39, 42).
- Trend Bar Failure is said to work from intraday to monthly charts, based on a few hand-picked examples (p. 37).
- "Capture High Profits" with low-volume pullbacks rests on one winner and one loser (pp. 56–59).
- Every chapter shows one winning and one losing example. There are no backtests, win rates or sample sizes anywhere in the book.
- PATI/MTT time-frame selection is a proprietary concept that the book never defines.
